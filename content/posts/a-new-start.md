Here I am, staring at neovim. I've finally given myself the space to set up a new blog and removed that roadblock to writing. And this is what's coming.

I used to start with a post about a blinking cursor. Cute, sure, but this seems more appropriate. Plus, my cursor doesn't blink any more.

There's not much of a plan for this blog. This isn't to get a job or prove I know what I'm talking about. Somewhere along the line I forgot the purpose of writing isn't the outcome.

Sure, if you write a book and it makes millions that's a pretty good outcome.

Writing is about exercising your thoughts. It's the journey. It is making tangible the intangible thoughts you have in your head, even if in the end they're only words on a page.

A family member of mine is in school and told me how pointless her essays were. She uses ChatGPT to complete them. I'm sure that many of the assignments are busy work, but I hate that she's missing the opportunity to deepen her learning by actively creating something with what she's read.

I recently read A Mind for Numbers by Dr Barbara Oakley. I wish I'd read it earlier. In giving the science behind learning and tools for doing so effectively, I see the why more clearly. Most of the advice and tools boil down to read, generate something with that knowledge, and take a break.

Usually the generative aspect is quizzing yourself or writing.

The other part that I've had trouble internalizing is "Give yourself permission to suck" which I remember from some Stephen Tyler (Aerosmith) interview. Who knows if it actually was him.

My weakness has been a fear of making mistakes. Of sucking at whatever I'm learning.

But everyone sucks when they start something. Some pick up things more quickly than others and that's usually due to things outside ability like interest and environment.

There's a Feynman quote I can't remember right now, but essentially there's something about "fast learners" are often times fast because they're interested in the thing they're learning. They get interested in something and just keep teasing out new things from it.

Now I've got a decent, if rambling, piece of streaming thoughts. Who needs to edit?
